## 1. Problem

A tenant bills its customers through Finaxis: it creates a charge over the API, shows its customer the QR code, and expects to be told, once and correctly, when the money arrives. Everything between those two moments happens outside the tenant's view and partly outside Finaxis's control. The payer's bank settles the payment through the rails whenever the payer acts: possibly after the charge expired, twice for the same code, or for a code someone altered. The rails then tell Finaxis at least once, possibly late, out of order, or only after an outage ends (rails simulator design, section 7).

Billing turns those facts into a charge's state and a ledger entry. Money that arrived is never lost and never counted twice: a duplicated notification has one effect, a payment the charge did not expect is still credited, and the charge and the ledger never disagree, because they change in one transaction (overview, section 7.2).

The overview fixes the shape: the charge's state machine, settlement and posting in one transaction, deduplication by `EndToEndId`, late payments credited and flagged, refunds as reversal entries, and a risk check before charging (sections 7.1–7.6). The [ledger](2026-11-18-ledger.md), [idempotency](2026-11-21-idempotency.md) and [rails simulator](2026-11-21-rails-simulator.md) designs fix the parts billing builds on. This document decides how billing joins them, from `POST /v1/charges` to the fault-injection suite.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- A tenant creates a Pix charge with one idempotent request and receives a BR Code that a payer's app can read and the simulator can pay.
- Every Pix credit the rails notify is recorded exactly once in the ledger, however often and in whatever order it is delivered, and whatever the state of its charge.
- A charge is paid only by a payment of its amount that the rails accepted before it expired; every other payment is credited to the tenant and flagged, never refused or lost.
- A charge, its payment and their ledger entry commit together or not at all.
- A refund never returns more than a payment brought in, and never takes a tenant's balance below zero.
- Each rule is enforced by the domain and, where it can be expressed, repeated by the database.

**Non-goals**

- Returning money through the rails. A refund is a ledger entry in phase 1; the Pix return (`pacs.004`) arrives with the payouts' messages in phase 2 (rails simulator design, section 2).
- Split, sub-accounts and platform fees (phase 2). Phase 1 settlement has two postings (demo script, scene 5).
- Boleto, Pix Automático, subscriptions and charges with a due date.
- Canceling a charge. The state arrives with its endpoint (section 3.4).
- A review workflow. A flagged payment is visible and refundable; the back office that works through flags comes later.
- Payer data. The core keeps no payer name or tax ID in phase 1 (section 7.2).

## 3. Model

### 3.1 Concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Charge** | A tenant's request to be paid an amount by Pix before a time. It owns its BR Code and its transaction ID, and its status says whether it was paid |
| **Transaction ID** | The 25 letters or digits that identify a charge on the rails (`txid`). The BR Code carries it and the credit notification returns it, so it is how a payment finds its charge |
| **BR Code** | The text of the QR code, in the EMV format of the Central Bank's BR Code manual (section 5) |
| **Payment** | One Pix credit received for a charge, identified by its `EndToEndId`. It is either the payment that settled the charge, or a flagged one |
| **Flagged payment** | A payment credited to the tenant that did not settle its charge: late, for a charge already paid, or of another amount. The tenant decides whether to refund it |
| **Refund** | Money given back from one payment, in full or in part, as a ledger entry that reverses part or all of the payment's entry |
| **Expiry** | The moment after which a payment no longer settles the charge. It is judged by when the rails accepted the payment, not by when the core heard of it (section 8.2) |

### 3.2 Charge states

```
            settled              refund < remaining           refund = remaining
  ACTIVE ───────────► PAID ─────────────────► PARTIALLY_REFUNDED ─────────────► REFUNDED
     │                 ▲  └──────────────────── refund = remaining ──────────────►┘
     │ expires         │ settled by a payment accepted before expiry
     ▼                 │
  EXPIRED ─────────────┘
```

| From | To | When |
|---|---|---|
| `ACTIVE` | `PAID` | A payment of the charge's amount, accepted before `expires_at` |
| `ACTIVE` | `EXPIRED` | `expires_at` has passed (section 9) |
| `EXPIRED` | `PAID` | A payment of the charge's amount, accepted before `expires_at` and notified after the charge expired |
| `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` | `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` | A refund of the settling payment leaves part of it |
| `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` | `REFUNDED` | A refund of the settling payment returns what remained of it |

- The domain's `Charge` exposes only these transitions, each as a method that checks its condition; there is no setter for the status, so an invalid transition cannot be written. The database repeats what it can: a `CHECK` ties `paid_at` to the paid states (section 11).
- `EXPIRED → PAID` is the honest outcome of a slow notification: the payer paid in time, and the rails took longer to say so. A tenant may see the charge expire and then be paid, and the API reference says that `paid` is final while `expired` is not.
- `REFUNDED` is final. `EXPIRED` is final for every payment accepted after `expires_at`.
- Refunds of flagged payments never change the charge's status: the status describes the settling payment.

### 3.3 Payment outcomes

Every payment is recorded with one outcome, decided when it is settled (section 8.2):

| Outcome | Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `settled` | — | The charge's one settling payment |
| `flagged` | `charge_already_paid` | Another payment had already settled the charge |
| `flagged` | `charge_expired` | Accepted at or after `expires_at` |
| `flagged` | `amount_mismatch` | Its amount differs from the charge's |

A flagged payment is credited to the tenant like a settled one: the money is in Finaxis's settlement account, so it is owed to the tenant (overview, section 7.6).

### 3.4 Refinements of the overview

| Overview | This design | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `CREATED → ACTIVE` (sections 7.1, 7.5) | A charge is created `ACTIVE`; there is no `CREATED` | It is created with its BR Code in one transaction, so `CREATED` could never be observed; a charge that risk refuses is never created (section 4.3) |
| `EXPIRED` and `CANCELED` are final (section 7.5) | `EXPIRED → PAID` for a payment accepted before expiry; `CANCELED` arrives with its endpoint | Lateness is judged by the rails' acceptance time (section 8.2); no state is built that nothing reaches |
| Late payment: "the charge is flagged for review and refund" (section 7.6) | The payment is flagged, with a reason; repeated payments and other amounts are flagged the same way | A charge can receive several payments, and each needs its own decision |
| Settlement: verify, deduplicate, then one transaction with `ChargePaid` in the outbox (section 7.2) | The callback is verified, recorded in an inbox and answered, then settled from the inbox; `ChargePaid` joins the transaction once the event registry exists (PR 1.44a) | The simulator design already has the core answer before settling (its section 5.3); no payment is lost if settlement fails |
| A refund is "a new entry with inverted postings" (section 6.4) | An entry that reverses part or all of one payment's entry, linked by `reverses_entry_id` | Partial refunds; a Pix return refers to one `EndToEndId` |
| A reversal has "the original's postings in the opposite direction" (ledger design, section 3.1) | A partial reversal has them in the opposite direction, for less than the original's amounts | `reverses_entry_id` links the two either way; the glossary's *Reversal* follows in PR 1.41a |
| `billing` depends on `ledger`, `rails`, `risk` and `split` (section 5.1) | `split` joins in phase 2; `rails` calls billing through a port it defines (section 7.3) | No dependency cycle between the two |
| "Legal and Pix receiver details (CNPJ, city) arrive with billing" (tenancy design, section 3.4) | The BR Code names the tenant's organization and the platform's city (section 5.3) | No billing profile is needed until a feature reads it |

## 4. Creating a charge

### 4.1 Request and response

```
POST /v1/charges
Idempotency-Key: 6f1c2a90-…

{
  "amount": 10000,
  "currency": "BRL",
  "description": "Mensalidade de novembro",
  "expires_in": 1800,
  "metadata": { "order_id": "ord_8842" }
}
```

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| `amount` | Required. Integer centavos, 1 to 100,000,000 (R$ 1,000,000.00); the tenant's own limits are risk's (section 6) |
| `currency` | Required. `BRL`, the only currency of Pix |
| `description` | Optional. 1 to 140 characters, for the tenant's records; not in the BR Code |
| `expires_in` | Optional. Seconds, 60 to 604,800 (7 days); 86,400 when omitted, the default of the Central Bank's Pix API |
| `metadata` | Optional. Up to 20 keys of 1 to 40 characters, with string values of up to 500; stored and returned, never read by Finaxis. The API reference says it must hold no personal data |

`201 Created`, with `Location: /v1/charges/ch_…`:

```json
{
  "id": "ch_01jdq8k3m4n5p6q7r8s9t0v1w2",
  "object": "charge",
  "status": "active",
  "amount": 10000,
  "currency": "BRL",
  "amount_refunded": 0,
  "description": "Mensalidade de novembro",
  "metadata": { "order_id": "ord_8842" },
  "pix": {
    "transaction_id": "fx7Kq2mP9wR4tY6uA8sD3fG5h",
    "br_code": "00020101021226…6304A1B2"
  },
  "expires_at": "2026-11-22T15:00:00.000Z",
  "paid_at": null,
  "created_at": "2026-11-22T14:30:00.000Z",
  "livemode": false
}
```

- `livemode` is the tenant's environment, so a client can tell test charges from live ones in its logs. It is derived from the tenant, never sent.
- Unknown fields are refused with `422 validation_failed` (PR 1.20g), so a misspelled `expires_at` is not silently ignored.
- The response is stored with the request's idempotency key, so a retry returns it byte for byte (idempotency design, section 3.4).

### 4.2 Refusals

| Refusal | Code |
|---|---|
| A field outside its rule, or an unknown field | `422 validation_failed`, with the field |
| A risk rule of the tenant (section 6) | `422 limit_exceeded`, with the rule |

### 4.3 Steps

`POST /v1/charges` requires `charges:write`. Authentication, rate limiting, the permission check and the idempotency key come first (idempotency design, section 4.3); the operation then runs inside the key's transaction:

1. **Validate** the body against the contract.
2. **Ask risk** (section 6). A refusal is returned as a value: nothing is written, and the refusal is stored with the key.
3. **Open the tenant's ledger account**, `tenant:{tenant_id}:available`, if it is not open yet. `openAccount` is idempotent, and opening it here means the account exists before any payment can arrive for it (section 8.3).
4. **Generate** the charge's ID, its transaction ID (section 5.2) and its BR Code (section 5.1).
5. **Insert** the charge, `ACTIVE`.

Steps 2 to 5 commit together with the key. Once the event registry exists, `ChargeCreated` is published in the same transaction (PR 1.44a).

## 5. BR Code

### 5.1 Payload

The core encodes the EMV *Merchant-Presented Mode* format of the Central Bank's BR Code manual: a sequence of fields, each a two-digit tag, a two-digit length and a value.

| Tag | Field | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `00` | Payload format indicator | `01` |
| `01` | Point of initiation method | `12`, a code for one payment |
| `26` | Merchant account information | `00` = `br.gov.bcb.pix`; `01` = the environment's Pix key (section 5.3) |
| `52` | Merchant category code | `0000` |
| `53` | Transaction currency | `986`, the real |
| `54` | Transaction amount | The amount in reais with two decimals and a point, such as `100.00` |
| `58` | Country code | `BR` |
| `59` | Merchant name | Section 5.3 |
| `60` | Merchant city | Section 5.3 |
| `62` | Additional data field | `05` = the transaction ID |
| `63` | CRC16 | CRC16-CCITT (polynomial `0x1021`, initial value `0xFFFF`) of everything before it, `6304` included, as four uppercase hexadecimal digits |

This is the code the simulator reads (rails simulator design, section 4.2): the amount and the transaction ID travel in the code instead of behind a location URL, a deviation that design lists (its section 9). The encoder is a pure function in billing's domain, tested against the examples of the BR Code manual. The fault-injection suite (PR 1.42) pays the codes through the simulator's own reader, so a code the core encodes wrongly is refused by an independent decoder rather than read back by the same mistake.

### 5.2 Transaction ID

25 characters drawn from `[A-Za-z0-9]` with `SecureRandom`, the most that subfield `05` of the BR Code holds: about 148 bits. It is not derived from the charge's ID, because a UUIDv7 would reveal when the charge was created and its 26 characters do not fit. A unique constraint across all tenants turns the improbable collision into a new draw. It must be unique across tenants because a credit notification names no tenant, only the transaction ID (section 7.3).

### 5.3 Merchant name, city and key

- **Name** (`59`, at most 25 characters): the tenant's organization name, with accents removed and anything other than letters, digits and spaces dropped, cut at 25. `TenantView` gains the organization's name for it.
- **City** (`60`, at most 15 characters): the platform's city, from configuration (`SAO PAULO`). Finaxis is the receiving participant, and tenants have no address in the system yet.
- **Key** (`26-01`): a random Pix key per environment, from configuration. It names Finaxis's account as the receiver, as an institution's own key does; the DICT is not simulated, so nothing resolves it.

The name and the city are what a payer's app shows before the payer confirms. A billing profile with the tenant's legal name, CNPJ and city replaces them when a feature needs it.

## 6. Risk rules

`risk` decides, before a charge exists, whether the tenant may create it. Its rules limit what an abused or broken integration can produce, not what a tenant may sell:

| Rule | Live | Test | Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amount` | R$ 10,000.00 per charge | R$ 1,000.00 per charge | — |
| `daily_amount` | R$ 200,000.00 of charges created | R$ 10,000.00 | The business date, in `America/Sao_Paulo` |
| `hourly_count` | 1,000 charges created | 200 | The clock hour, in UTC |

```java
public interface RiskApi {
    ChargeDecision assessCharge(TenantId tenant, Money amount);   // Allowed or Denied(rule)
}
```

- The values are configuration per environment, revised with the load tests (PR 1.70); values per tenant come with the back office.
- `amount` is a comparison. The other two are counters in risk's own table, one row per tenant, rule and window, changed with the kind of conditional statement the ledger uses for balances (ledger design, section 5.2): `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` creates the window's row, then `UPDATE … SET total = total + :amount, count = count + 1 WHERE … AND total + :amount <= :max_total AND count + 1 <= :max_count` adds the charge. One row updated is `Allowed`; none is `Denied`. Two concurrent charges cannot both take the last unit of a limit, and nothing is retried.
- The call joins the charge's transaction, so a charge that fails after risk allowed it gives back its share of the counters, and a denied charge leaves them as they were.
- The counters count charges created, not money received: they limit what a tenant can ask for, when it asks. A daily job removes windows older than 7 days.
- `risk` depends on `shared` only. Reading the ledger, which the overview allows (section 5.1), arrives with the first rule that needs a balance.
