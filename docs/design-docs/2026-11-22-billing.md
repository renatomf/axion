## 1. Problem

A tenant bills its customers through Axion: it creates a charge over the API, shows its customer the QR code, and expects to be told, once and correctly, when the money arrives. Everything between those two moments happens outside the tenant's view and partly outside Axion's control. The payer's bank settles the payment through the rails whenever the payer acts: possibly after the charge expired, twice for the same code, or for a code someone altered. The rails then tell Axion at least once, possibly late, out of order, or only after an outage ends (rails simulator design, section 7).

Billing turns those facts into a charge's state and a ledger entry. Money that arrived is never lost and never counted twice: a duplicated notification has one effect, a payment the charge did not expect is still credited, and the charge and the ledger never disagree, because they change in one transaction (overview, section 7.2).

The overview fixes the shape: the charge's state machine, settlement and posting in one transaction, deduplication by `EndToEndId`, late payments credited and flagged, refunds as reversal entries, and a risk check before charging (sections 7.1–7.6). The [ledger](2026-11-18-ledger.md), [idempotency](2026-11-21-idempotency.md) and [rails simulator](2026-11-21-rails-simulator.md) designs fix the parts billing builds on. This document decides how billing joins them, from `POST /v1/charges` to the fault-injection suite.

## 2. Goals and non-goals

**Goals**

- A tenant creates a Pix charge with one idempotent request and receives a BR Code that a payer's app can read and the simulator can pay.
- Every Pix credit the rails notify is recorded exactly once in the ledger, however often and in whatever order it is delivered, and whatever the state of its charge.
- A charge is paid only by a payment of its amount that the rails accepted before it expired; every other payment is credited to the tenant and flagged, never refused or lost.
- A charge, its payment and their ledger entry commit together or not at all.
- A refund never returns more than a payment brought in, and never takes a tenant's balance below zero.
- Each rule is enforced by the domain and, where it can be expressed, repeated by the database.

**Non-goals**

- Returning money through the rails. A refund is a ledger entry in phase 1; the Pix return (`pacs.004`) arrives with the payouts' messages in phase 2 (rails simulator design, section 2).
- Split, sub-accounts and platform fees (phase 2). Phase 1 settlement has two postings (demo script, scene 5).
- Boleto, Pix Automático, subscriptions and charges with a due date.
- Canceling a charge. The state arrives with its endpoint (section 3.4).
- A review workflow. A flagged payment is visible and refundable; the back office that works through flags comes later.
- Payer data. The core keeps no payer name or tax ID in phase 1 (section 7.2).

## 3. Model

### 3.1 Concepts

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Charge** | A tenant's request to be paid an amount by Pix before a time. It owns its BR Code and its transaction ID, and its status says whether it was paid |
| **Transaction ID** | The 25 letters or digits that identify a charge on the rails (`txid`). The BR Code carries it and the credit notification returns it, so it is how a payment finds its charge |
| **BR Code** | The text of the QR code, in the EMV format of the Central Bank's BR Code manual (section 5) |
| **Payment** | One Pix credit received for a charge, identified by its `EndToEndId`. It is either the payment that settled the charge, or a flagged one |
| **Flagged payment** | A payment credited to the tenant that did not settle its charge: late, for a charge already paid, or of another amount. The tenant decides whether to refund it |
| **Refund** | Money given back from one payment, in full or in part, as a ledger entry that reverses part or all of the payment's entry |
| **Expiry** | The moment after which a payment no longer settles the charge. It is judged by when the rails accepted the payment, not by when the core heard of it (section 8.2) |

### 3.2 Charge states

```
            settled              refund < remaining           refund = remaining
  ACTIVE ───────────► PAID ─────────────────► PARTIALLY_REFUNDED ─────────────► REFUNDED
     │                 ▲  └──────────────────── refund = remaining ──────────────►┘
     │ expires         │ settled by a payment accepted before expiry
     ▼                 │
  EXPIRED ─────────────┘
```

| From | To | When |
|---|---|---|
| `ACTIVE` | `PAID` | A payment of the charge's amount, accepted before `expires_at` |
| `ACTIVE` | `EXPIRED` | `expires_at` has passed (section 9) |
| `EXPIRED` | `PAID` | A payment of the charge's amount, accepted before `expires_at` and notified after the charge expired |
| `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` | `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` | A refund of the settling payment leaves part of it |
| `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` | `REFUNDED` | A refund of the settling payment returns what remained of it |

- The domain's `Charge` exposes only these transitions, each as a method that checks its condition; there is no setter for the status, so an invalid transition cannot be written. The database repeats what it can: a `CHECK` ties `paid_at` to the paid states (section 11).
- `EXPIRED → PAID` is the honest outcome of a slow notification: the payer paid in time, and the rails took longer to say so. A tenant may see the charge expire and then be paid, and the API reference says that `paid` is final while `expired` is not.
- `REFUNDED` is final. `EXPIRED` is final for every payment accepted after `expires_at`.
- Refunds of flagged payments never change the charge's status: the status describes the settling payment.

### 3.3 Payment outcomes

Every payment is recorded with one outcome, decided when it is settled (section 8.2):

| Outcome | Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `settled` | — | The charge's one settling payment |
| `flagged` | `charge_already_paid` | Another payment had already settled the charge |
| `flagged` | `charge_expired` | Accepted at or after `expires_at` |
| `flagged` | `amount_mismatch` | Its amount differs from the charge's |

A flagged payment is credited to the tenant like a settled one: the money is in Axion's settlement account, so it is owed to the tenant (overview, section 7.6).

### 3.4 Refinements of the overview

| Overview | This design | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| `CREATED → ACTIVE` (sections 7.1, 7.5) | A charge is created `ACTIVE`; there is no `CREATED` | It is created with its BR Code in one transaction, so `CREATED` could never be observed; a charge that risk refuses is never created (section 4.3) |
| `EXPIRED` and `CANCELED` are final (section 7.5) | `EXPIRED → PAID` for a payment accepted before expiry; `CANCELED` arrives with its endpoint | Lateness is judged by the rails' acceptance time (section 8.2); no state is built that nothing reaches |
| Late payment: "the charge is flagged for review and refund" (section 7.6) | The payment is flagged, with a reason; repeated payments and other amounts are flagged the same way | A charge can receive several payments, and each needs its own decision |
| Settlement: verify, deduplicate, then one transaction with `ChargePaid` in the outbox (section 7.2) | The callback is verified, recorded in an inbox and answered, then settled from the inbox; `ChargePaid` joins the transaction once the event registry exists (PR 1.44a) | The simulator design already has the core answer before settling (its section 5.3); no payment is lost if settlement fails |
| A refund is "a new entry with inverted postings" (section 6.4) | An entry that reverses part or all of one payment's entry, linked by `reverses_entry_id` | Partial refunds; a Pix return refers to one `EndToEndId` |
| A reversal has "the original's postings in the opposite direction" (ledger design, section 3.1) | A partial reversal has them in the opposite direction, for less than the original's amounts | `reverses_entry_id` links the two either way; the glossary's *Reversal* follows in PR 1.41a |
| `billing` depends on `ledger`, `rails`, `risk` and `split` (section 5.1) | `split` joins in phase 2; `rails` calls billing through a port it defines (section 7.3) | No dependency cycle between the two |
| "Legal and Pix receiver details (CNPJ, city) arrive with billing" (tenancy design, section 3.4) | The BR Code names the tenant's organization and the platform's city (section 5.3) | No billing profile is needed until a feature reads it |

## 4. Creating a charge

### 4.1 Request and response

```
POST /v1/charges
Idempotency-Key: 6f1c2a90-…

{
  "amount": 10000,
  "currency": "BRL",
  "description": "Mensalidade de novembro",
  "expires_in": 1800,
  "metadata": { "order_id": "ord_8842" }
}
```

| Field | Rule |
|---|---|
| `amount` | Required. Integer centavos, 1 to 100,000,000 (R$ 1,000,000.00); the tenant's own limits are risk's (section 6) |
| `currency` | Required. `BRL`, the only currency of Pix |
| `description` | Optional. 1 to 140 characters, for the tenant's records; not in the BR Code |
| `expires_in` | Optional. Seconds, 60 to 604,800 (7 days); 86,400 when omitted, the default of the Central Bank's Pix API |
| `metadata` | Optional. Up to 20 keys of 1 to 40 characters, with string values of up to 500; stored and returned, never read by Axion. The API reference says it must hold no personal data |

`201 Created`, with `Location: /v1/charges/ch_…`:

```json
{
  "id": "ch_01jdq8k3m4n5p6q7r8s9t0v1w2",
  "object": "charge",
  "status": "active",
  "amount": 10000,
  "currency": "BRL",
  "amount_refunded": 0,
  "description": "Mensalidade de novembro",
  "metadata": { "order_id": "ord_8842" },
  "pix": {
    "transaction_id": "fx7Kq2mP9wR4tY6uA8sD3fG5h",
    "br_code": "00020101021226…6304A1B2"
  },
  "expires_at": "2026-11-22T15:00:00.000Z",
  "paid_at": null,
  "created_at": "2026-11-22T14:30:00.000Z",
  "livemode": false
}
```

- `livemode` is the tenant's environment, so a client can tell test charges from live ones in its logs. It is derived from the tenant, never sent.
- Unknown fields are refused with `422 validation_failed` (PR 1.20g), so a misspelled `expires_at` is not silently ignored.
- The response is stored with the request's idempotency key, so a retry returns it byte for byte (idempotency design, section 3.4).

### 4.2 Refusals

| Refusal | Code |
|---|---|
| A field outside its rule, or an unknown field | `422 validation_failed`, with the field |
| A risk rule of the tenant (section 6) | `422 limit_exceeded`, with the rule |

### 4.3 Steps

`POST /v1/charges` requires `charges:write`. Authentication, rate limiting, the permission check and the idempotency key come first (idempotency design, section 4.3); the operation then runs inside the key's transaction:

1. **Validate** the body against the contract.
2. **Ask risk** (section 6). A refusal is returned as a value: nothing is written, and the refusal is stored with the key.
3. **Open the tenant's ledger account**, `tenant:{tenant_id}:available`, if it is not open yet. `openAccount` is idempotent, and opening it here means the account exists before any payment can arrive for it (section 8.3).
4. **Generate** the charge's ID, its transaction ID (section 5.2) and its BR Code (section 5.1).
5. **Insert** the charge, `ACTIVE`.

Steps 2 to 5 commit together with the key. Once the event registry exists, `ChargeCreated` is published in the same transaction (PR 1.44a).

## 5. BR Code

### 5.1 Payload

The core encodes the EMV *Merchant-Presented Mode* format of the Central Bank's BR Code manual: a sequence of fields, each a two-digit tag, a two-digit length and a value.

| Tag | Field | Value |
|---|---|---|
| `00` | Payload format indicator | `01` |
| `01` | Point of initiation method | `12`, a code for one payment |
| `26` | Merchant account information | `00` = `br.gov.bcb.pix`; `01` = the environment's Pix key (section 5.3) |
| `52` | Merchant category code | `0000` |
| `53` | Transaction currency | `986`, the real |
| `54` | Transaction amount | The amount in reais with two decimals and a point, such as `100.00` |
| `58` | Country code | `BR` |
| `59` | Merchant name | Section 5.3 |
| `60` | Merchant city | Section 5.3 |
| `62` | Additional data field | `05` = the transaction ID |
| `63` | CRC16 | CRC16-CCITT (polynomial `0x1021`, initial value `0xFFFF`) of everything before it, `6304` included, as four uppercase hexadecimal digits |

This is the code the simulator reads (rails simulator design, section 4.2): the amount and the transaction ID travel in the code instead of behind a location URL, a deviation that design lists (its section 9). The encoder is a pure function in billing's domain, tested against the examples of the BR Code manual. The fault-injection suite (PR 1.42) pays the codes through the simulator's own reader, so a code the core encodes wrongly is refused by an independent decoder rather than read back by the same mistake.

### 5.2 Transaction ID

25 characters drawn from `[A-Za-z0-9]` with `SecureRandom`, the most that subfield `05` of the BR Code holds: about 148 bits. It is not derived from the charge's ID, because a UUIDv7 would reveal when the charge was created and its 26 characters do not fit. A unique constraint across all tenants turns the improbable collision into a new draw. It must be unique across tenants because a credit notification names no tenant, only the transaction ID (section 7.3).

### 5.3 Merchant name, city and key

- **Name** (`59`, at most 25 characters): the tenant's organization name, with accents removed and anything other than ASCII letters, digits and spaces dropped, cut at 25. A name with nothing left, such as one written only in another script, becomes `AXION`, the receiving participant. `TenantView` gains the organization's name for it.
- **City** (`60`, at most 15 characters): the platform's city, from configuration (`SAO PAULO`). Axion is the receiving participant, and tenants have no address in the system yet.
- **Key** (`26-01`): a random Pix key per environment, from configuration. It names Axion's account as the receiver, as an institution's own key does; the DICT is not simulated, so nothing resolves it.

The name and the city are what a payer's app shows before the payer confirms. A billing profile with the tenant's legal name, CNPJ and city replaces them when a feature needs it.

## 6. Risk rules

`risk` decides, before a charge exists, whether the tenant may create it. Its rules limit what an abused or broken integration can produce, not what a tenant may sell:

| Rule | Live | Test | Window |
|---|---|---|---|
| `amount` | R$ 10,000.00 per charge | R$ 1,000.00 per charge | — |
| `daily_amount` | R$ 200,000.00 of charges created | R$ 10,000.00 | The business date, in `America/Sao_Paulo` |
| `hourly_count` | 1,000 charges created | 200 | The clock hour, in UTC |

```java
public interface RiskApi {
    ChargeDecision assessCharge(TenantId tenant, Money amount);   // Allowed or Denied(rule)
}
```

- The values are configuration per environment, revised with the load tests (PR 1.70); values per tenant come with the back office.
- `amount` is a comparison. The other two are counters in risk's own table, one row per tenant, rule and window, changed with the kind of conditional statement the ledger uses for balances (ledger design, section 5.2): `INSERT … ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING` creates the window's row, then `UPDATE … SET total = total + :amount, count = count + 1 WHERE … AND total + :amount <= :max_total AND count + 1 <= :max_count` adds the charge. One row updated is `Allowed`; none is `Denied`. Two concurrent charges cannot both take the last unit of a limit, and nothing is retried.
- The call joins the charge's transaction, so a charge that fails after risk allowed it gives back its share of the counters, and a denied charge leaves them as they were.
- The counters count charges created, not money received: they limit what a tenant can ask for, when it asks. A daily job removes windows older than 7 days.
- `risk` depends on `shared` only. Reading the ledger, which the overview allows (section 5.1), arrives with the first rule that needs a balance.

## 7. Receiving payments

### 7.1 The callback endpoint

The simulator delivers each credit notification (`pacs.008`) to `POST /rails/pix/credits` (rails simulator design, sections 5 and 6). The `rails` module owns the endpoint, on a filter chain of its own: it is neither `/v1`, with API keys, nor `/internal`, with tokens, and the public reverse proxy does not route it. The endpoint checks, in order, and stops at the first failure:

1. The body is at most 16 KiB, read in full; otherwise `413`.
2. `Content-Digest` matches the SHA-256 of the body.
3. `Signature-Input` covers exactly `"@method" "@path" "content-type" "content-digest"`, with `alg="ed25519"` and a `keyid` the core is configured with, and `Signature` verifies with that key.
4. `created` is at most 5 minutes old and at most 1 minute ahead of the core's clock.
5. The body parses, and every field the core reads is well formed: `messageType` is `pacs.008`, `localInstrument` is `QRDN`, `creditorAgent.ispb` is Axion's, the currency is `BRL`, the amount is a positive decimal with two fraction digits, and the `EndToEndId` and the transaction ID have their formats.

Failures 2 to 4 get `401` with no detail; failure 5 gets `400`, which the simulator keeps for manual redelivery. Only then is the credit recorded (section 7.2), and answered `202 Accepted`, a duplicate included. The answer comes before the charge is settled, so a settlement that fails is retried by the core, never by the rails (rails simulator design, section 5.3).

Requests are rate-limited per signing key, 100 per second with bursts of 200, the budget of a live tenant (tenancy design, section 6); the simulator honors `429` and its `Retry-After`.

### 7.2 The inbox

```
rails.pix_credits
  end_to_end_id     char(32) primary key     -- format of the rails simulator design, section 4.3 (CHECK)
  message_id        char(32) not null
  transaction_id    text not null            -- 1–25 letters or digits (CHECK)
  amount_minor      bigint not null          -- > 0 (CHECK)
  currency          char(3) not null         -- BRL (CHECK)
  accepted_at       timestamptz not null     -- acceptanceDateTime: when the rails settled the payment
  debtor_ispb       char(8) not null
  key_id            text not null            -- the signing key that verified it
  content_digest    bytea not null           -- SHA-256 of the body, 32 bytes (CHECK)
  received_at       timestamptz not null
  status            text not null            -- received | processed | unmatched (CHECK)
  attempts          integer not null
  next_attempt_at   timestamptz null         -- set only while received (CHECK)
  processed_at      timestamptz null
  index (next_attempt_at) where status = 'received'
```

- The credit is inserted with `ON CONFLICT (end_to_end_id) DO NOTHING`, so a duplicated notification is recorded once: the first of the three barriers of section 8.4.
- The inbox keeps what the core needs and the evidence of what it accepted: the `messageId`, the `keyid` and the digest of the exact bytes. It keeps neither the payer's name nor their tax ID, and neither does billing. The simulator keeps the full message, and the digest proves which one the core accepted (section 15).
- The table holds no tenant: the tenant is not known until the transaction ID is resolved (section 7.3). It has no tenant policy and is reached only through `rails` and its role, `axion_rails`, as the tenancy directory is reached only through `tenancy` (tenancy design, section 5.4).

### 7.3 Processing

A processor in `rails` hands what the inbox holds to billing, at least once:

1. Every 500 ms, claim up to 16 `received` credits whose `next_attempt_at` has passed, with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, and move their `next_attempt_at` 30 seconds ahead as a lease, in one short transaction. A credit is inserted due at once.
2. For each, translate the row into a `PixCredit`, with the `EndToEndId`, the transaction ID, the amount as `Money`, the acceptance time, the payer's institution and the `messageId`, and pass it to the `PixCreditHandler` port, which `rails` defines in its API and `billing` implements.
3. Record the result in the inbox: `processed` for `Recorded` and `AlreadyRecorded`, `unmatched` for `UnknownTransaction`.

```java
public interface PixCreditHandler {
    CreditResult handle(PixCredit credit);   // Recorded, AlreadyRecorded or UnknownTransaction
}
```

- `rails` never imports billing: it calls a port it owns, and billing depends on `rails` to implement it, the direction of the overview's module map. The rails' format stops at the port: billing sees `Money` and a transaction ID, never ISO 20022 names.
- Billing resolves the tenant from the transaction ID before it opens its transaction, through `billing.find_charge_tenant(transaction_id)`, a `SECURITY DEFINER` function that returns the tenant of the one charge with that ID and nothing else, as the API key lookup does (tenancy design, section 5.4). It then binds that tenant and runs the settlement (section 8).
- A crash between steps 2 and 3 leaves a credit that is handled again when its lease expires, and billing answers `AlreadyRecorded` (section 8.4). An exception counts an attempt and retries the credit with the simulator's backoff, at most 5 minutes apart, and never gives it up: money that arrived must be recorded. From the fifth failed attempt each failure is logged as an error, and `axion_rails_credit_oldest_pending_seconds` is the alert's signal.
- An `unmatched` credit names a transaction ID that no charge has. On these rails it can only come from a code the core did not issue, paid through the simulator by hand. Its money is in the settlement account and in no tenant's balance: it is logged as an error with its `EndToEndId`, counted, and left to an operator, whose return to the payer comes with the rails' returns (phase 2). Reconciliation against the rails' statement (phase 4) is the control that finds it otherwise.

## 8. Settlement

### 8.1 One transaction

`PixCreditHandler.handle`, in billing, runs one transaction with the charge's tenant bound:

1. **Lock the charge** by its transaction ID, `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`. The tenant's policy applies, and the charge, the caller's own row, is locked before the ledger's, as the ledger's lock order asks (ledger design, section 5.2).
2. **Look for the payment** by its `EndToEndId`. If it exists, return `AlreadyRecorded`: nothing else happens.
3. **Decide the outcome** in the domain, with `charge.receive(payment)` (section 8.2). If the charge's expiry has passed and the expiry job has not reached it yet, the domain expires it first.
4. **Post the entry** (section 8.3). The ledger returns `Posted`. `AlreadyPosted` cannot happen without the payment of step 2, so it is treated as a bug.
5. **Insert the payment**, with its outcome, its reason and the entry's ID, and **update the charge**.
6. **Commit.** Once the registry exists, `ChargePaid` or `PaymentFlagged` is published in this transaction (PR 1.44a).

The charge, the payment and the entry commit together. A failure at any step, the ledger's balance check at commit included, rolls back all three, and the credit stays `received` to be handled again: nothing is half-posted (overview, section 7.6).

### 8.2 Deciding the outcome

The first row that matches decides:

| Charge | Payment | Outcome | Charge after |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PAID`, `PARTIALLY_REFUNDED` or `REFUNDED` | Any | `flagged`, `charge_already_paid` | Unchanged |
| `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` | Accepted at or after `expires_at` | `flagged`, `charge_expired` | `EXPIRED` |
| `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` | Of another amount | `flagged`, `amount_mismatch` | Unchanged |
| `ACTIVE` or `EXPIRED` | Of the charge's amount, accepted before `expires_at` | `settled` | `PAID`, with `paid_at` the acceptance time |

The acceptance time is `acceptanceDateTime`, when the rails settled the payment, signed by the rails with the message. It decides lateness, not the time the core heard of the payment: a payment that the `delay` fault, an outage or a day of retries brings late was still made in time. The core's clock enters only through `expires_at`, which the core set. The simulator's clock and the core's may differ slightly, which can move a payment made in the last instant of a charge to either side; every charge lasts at least a minute, so that edge stays an edge.

### 8.3 Ledger entries

| Entry | Tenant | Source: type, ID, purpose | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A payment, settled or flagged | The charge's | `pix_credit`, the `EndToEndId`, `settlement` | `platform:settlement` | `tenant:{id}:available` |
| A refund | The charge's | `refund`, the refund's ID, `refund` | `tenant:{id}:available` | `platform:settlement` |

- Each has two postings of the payment's or the refund's amount.
- A payment's source is its `EndToEndId`, unique on the rails, so the ledger's own key (invariant I4) refuses a second entry for one payment whatever billing does.
- A refund's entry sets `reverses_entry_id` to its payment's entry: it reverses all or part of it.
- `effective_date` is the business date of the acceptance time, or of the refund's creation. The correlation ID is the credit's `messageId`, which the simulator sends as `X-Correlation-Id`, or the refund request's.
- `platform:settlement` is the environment's: a hot asset that allows a negative balance (ledger design, section 5.3). Row-Level Security lets a module open only the bound tenant's accounts, so billing opens the two platform accounts at startup, each with its platform tenant bound, and keeps their IDs; it opens a tenant's account at the tenant's first charge (section 4.3), and finds it again by `openAccount` when it posts.

### 8.4 One effect

A credit delivered many times, by the simulator's retries, by its `duplicate` fault or after a lost acknowledgment, meets three barriers. Each alone gives one effect:

| Barrier | Table | Stops |
|---|---|---|
| The inbox's primary key, `end_to_end_id` | `rails.pix_credits` | A duplicated notification becoming a second credit to process |
| `unique (end_to_end_id)` on payments, behind the charge's lock | `billing.payments` | One credit processed twice, after a lease expired or a crash before it was marked |
| The ledger's source key | `ledger.entries` | A second entry for one payment, whatever billing does |

Two credits of one charge processed at the same time serialize on the charge's lock: the second sees the first's payment and, if the charge is now paid, is flagged `charge_already_paid`. Of two equal payments, the one processed first settles the charge; both are credited, so the tenant loses nothing either way.

## 9. Expiry

A job expires charges every 30 seconds, tenant by tenant. It finds the tenants with due charges, then expires each tenant's in batches:

```sql
UPDATE billing.charges SET status = 'EXPIRED', updated_at = :now
 WHERE id IN (SELECT id FROM billing.charges
               WHERE tenant_id = :tenant AND status = 'ACTIVE' AND expires_at <= :now
               ORDER BY expires_at
               LIMIT 500
               FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED)
RETURNING id
```

- It repeats the statement until a batch updates fewer than 500 rows, each batch in its own transaction with the tenant bound. `:now` comes from the injected `Clock`.
- Each batch publishes the `ChargeExpired` of every charge it expired, in its transaction (events and webhooks design, section 3.3). The event carries the charge with its payments and refunds, which billing's role reads with the tenant bound: that is why a transaction holds one tenant's charges.
- A tenant whose charges fail to expire is logged and left to the next run; it never holds back the others.
- `SKIP LOCKED` leaves a charge being settled to the next pass. Settlement expires a charge itself when its expiry is due (section 8.1), so the two never conflict.
- The job spans tenants, so it finds them and expires their charges as `axion_billing_jobs`, whose policies let it change only `ACTIVE` charges and only to `EXPIRED` (`USING (status = 'ACTIVE') WITH CHECK (status = 'EXPIRED')`), as the idempotency jobs role sees only expired keys (idempotency design, section 6.2). An `UPDATE` that reads the table checks the new row against the `SELECT` policies too, so the role sees `ACTIVE` and `EXPIRED` charges, never a paid one. Its grants are by column: it reads `id`, `tenant_id`, `status` and `expires_at`, and writes `status` and `updated_at`.
- A charge can read `active` for up to 30 seconds after its `expires_at`. The API returns `expires_at` with it, and the outcome of a payment never depends on the job (section 8.2).

## 10. Refunds

### 10.1 Request

```
POST /v1/charges/ch_…/refunds
Idempotency-Key: 0b9e4d17-…

{ "amount": 5000, "payment": "pmt_01jdq9a2b3c4d5e6f7g8h9j0k1" }
```

- Requires `refunds:write`, which admins and finance members hold, and the API keys they issue with it (tenancy design, section 3.3).
- `amount` is optional; when omitted, the refund returns all that remains of the payment.
- `payment` is optional when the charge has one payment and required when it has several, so a refund never guesses which payer gets money back.

`201 Created`, with no `Location`: a refund is read with its charge (section 12.2):

```json
{
  "id": "re_01jdqb7c8d9e0f1g2h3j4k5m6n",
  "object": "refund",
  "charge": "ch_01jdq8k3m4n5p6q7r8s9t0v1w2",
  "payment": "pmt_01jdq9a2b3c4d5e6f7g8h9j0k1",
  "amount": 5000,
  "status": "succeeded",
  "created_at": "2026-11-22T16:10:00.000Z"
}
```

### 10.2 Rules

| Refusal | Code |
|---|---|
| The charge does not exist, or is another tenant's | `404 not_found` |
| The charge has no payment | `422 charge_not_paid` |
| The charge has several payments and the request names none | `422 payment_required` |
| The named payment is not one of the charge's | `422 validation_failed`, with `payment` |
| The amount is more than remains of the payment | `422 amount_exceeds_refundable` |
| The payment was accepted more than 90 days ago | `422 refund_window_closed` |
| The charge already has 50 refunds | `422 refund_limit_reached` |
| The tenant's available balance is less than the amount | `422 insufficient_balance` |

The 90 days are the period the Pix rules give a receiver to return a payment. A tenant's balance can fall below a refund once payouts exist (phase 2); in phase 1 it only grows between refunds, but the rule belongs to the ledger, so billing reports the ledger's `InsufficientFunds` rather than repeat the check.

### 10.3 Steps

Inside the key's transaction:

1. **Lock the charge**, `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, then read its payments and refunds. Two refunds of one charge serialize here, so they cannot both take the last centavos of a payment.
2. **Check** the rules of section 10.2 in the domain, with `charge.refund(payment, amount)`.
3. **Post the entry** (section 8.3). On `InsufficientFunds` the ledger has rolled back to its savepoint and billing has written nothing yet, so the refusal is returned and stored with the key.
4. **Insert the refund**, `succeeded`, and add its amount to the payment's `amount_refunded`. If the payment settled the charge, update the charge's `amount_refunded` and its status (section 3.2).

A refund succeeds at once in phase 1, because it is only a ledger entry. Phase 2 sends it to the rails as a Pix return and adds `pending` and `failed`, with the money held in between.

## 11. Schema

The `billing` and `risk` schemas follow the conventions of the other modules: UUIDv7 keys, `timestamptz`, money as `bigint` minor units, enumerations as `text` with a `CHECK`, a forced tenant policy on every table (tenancy design, section 5.4), and access through `JdbcClient`.

```
billing.charges
  id                uuid primary key
  tenant_id         uuid not null
  status            text not null            -- ACTIVE | EXPIRED | PAID | PARTIALLY_REFUNDED | REFUNDED (CHECK)
  amount_minor      bigint not null          -- 1 to 100,000,000 (CHECK)
  currency          char(3) not null         -- BRL (CHECK)
  amount_refunded   bigint not null          -- of the settling payment; 0 to amount_minor (CHECK)
  description       text null                -- 1–140 characters (CHECK)
  metadata          jsonb not null           -- an object, {} when none (CHECK)
  transaction_id    text not null unique     -- 25 letters or digits (CHECK)
  br_code           text not null
  expires_at        timestamptz not null     -- at least a minute after created_at (CHECK)
  paid_at           timestamptz null         -- set exactly in the paid states (CHECK)
  created_at        timestamptz not null
  updated_at        timestamptz not null
  index (tenant_id, id)
  index (tenant_id, status, id)
  index (expires_at) where status = 'ACTIVE'

billing.payments
  id                uuid primary key
  tenant_id         uuid not null
  charge_id         uuid not null references charges
  end_to_end_id     char(32) not null unique
  amount_minor      bigint not null          -- > 0 (CHECK)
  amount_refunded   bigint not null          -- 0 to amount_minor (CHECK)
  accepted_at       timestamptz not null
  outcome           text not null            -- SETTLED | FLAGGED (CHECK)
  review_reason     text null                -- CHARGE_ALREADY_PAID | CHARGE_EXPIRED | AMOUNT_MISMATCH, set exactly when FLAGGED (CHECK)
  entry_id          uuid not null            -- the payment's entry in the ledger
  created_at        timestamptz not null
  index (charge_id)
  unique index (charge_id) where outcome = 'SETTLED'

billing.refunds
  id                uuid primary key
  tenant_id         uuid not null
  charge_id         uuid not null references charges
  payment_id        uuid not null references payments
  amount_minor      bigint not null          -- > 0 (CHECK)
  status            text not null            -- SUCCEEDED (CHECK)
  entry_id          uuid not null
  correlation_id    text not null
  created_at        timestamptz not null
  index (charge_id)
  index (payment_id)

risk.charge_volume
  tenant_id         uuid not null
  rule              text not null            -- DAILY_AMOUNT | HOURLY_COUNT (CHECK)
  window_start      timestamptz not null
  total_minor       bigint not null          -- >= 0 (CHECK)
  count             integer not null         -- >= 0 (CHECK)
  primary key (tenant_id, rule, window_start)
```

- The partial unique index on payments makes "one settling payment per charge" a rule the database holds as well as the domain.
- `entry_id` links a payment or a refund to its ledger entry by ID only: there are no foreign keys across schemas (overview, section 5.2).
- `metadata` is the only `jsonb` column; it is stored and returned, never queried.
- Roles: `axion_billing` may `SELECT`, `INSERT` and `UPDATE` charges and payments, and only `SELECT` and `INSERT` refunds, which never change; `axion_billing_jobs` expires charges (section 9); `axion_billing_lookup` owns the transaction ID lookup (section 7.3); `axion_risk` owns the counters, and `axion_risk_jobs` removes old windows; `axion_rails` owns the inbox. No role may `DELETE` from billing.

## 12. API

### 12.1 Modules

```java
public interface BillingApi {
    CreateChargeResult createCharge(ChargeRequest request);              // Created or Refused(rule)
    Optional<ChargeView> charge(ChargeId charge);
    Page<ChargeView> charges(ChargeFilter filter, Cursor cursor, int limit);
    RefundResult refund(ChargeId charge, RefundRequest request);         // Refunded or Refused(code)
}
```

- The tenant is the bound one, as in every module API (tenancy design, section 5.3); an ID of another tenant's charge is empty, exactly like an unknown one.
- Refusals are values, like the ledger's `PostResult`; exceptions are bugs and infrastructure failures (overview, section 19.1).
- Billing also implements `PixCreditHandler` for `rails` (section 7.3). No other module calls billing in phase 1.
- Public IDs: `ch_` for charges, `pmt_` for payments, `re_` for refunds.

### 12.2 HTTP

Contract-first, in `v1.yaml`:

| Operation | Scope | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `POST /v1/charges` | `charges:write` | Section 4 |
| `GET /v1/charges` | `charges:read` | Newest first; a cursor on the charge's ID; 1 to 100 per page; filters on `status` and on a creation range, `created[gte]` and `created[lt]` |
| `GET /v1/charges/{id}` | `charges:read` | The charge, with its `payments` and its `refunds` |
| `POST /v1/charges/{id}/refunds` | `refunds:write` | Section 10 |

- Another tenant's charge is `404`, like an unknown one (overview, section 4.3); the tenant isolation harness (PR 1.23) covers each operation as it is added.
- A payment, as the API shows it: `id`, `end_to_end_id`, `amount`, `amount_refunded`, `accepted_at`, `outcome` and `review_reason`. The detail embeds at most the first 100 payments; a charge paid more often than that is an anomaly, and a list of a charge's payments is the path if one ever needs it. A charge has at most 50 refunds (section 10.2).
- `POST /rails/pix/credits` is not in `v1.yaml`: its contract is the simulator's `rails.yaml`, which the core's tests read (rails simulator design, section 5.1).

### 12.3 Events

Once the registry exists (PR 1.44a), billing publishes `ChargeCreated`, `ChargePaid`, `ChargeExpired`, `PaymentFlagged` and `ChargeRefunded`, each in the transaction of its change. Their webhook names and payloads belong to the events and webhooks design (PR 1.43).

### 12.4 Observability

Metrics: `axion_billing_charges_created_total`, `axion_billing_payments_total{outcome,reason}`, `axion_billing_refunds_total`, `axion_billing_settlement_lag_seconds` (a histogram, from acceptance to settlement), `axion_risk_denials_total{rule}`, `axion_rails_credits_received_total{result}` (`new`, `duplicate`, `rejected`) and `axion_rails_credit_oldest_pending_seconds`. Logs carry the charge's, payment's and refund's IDs, the `EndToEndId` and the `messageId`; never a BR Code, metadata or payer data.

### 13.1 Fault-injection suite

PR 1.42 runs the simulator's image with Testcontainers, pays BR Codes the core issued, and drives the faults through the chaos API (rails simulator design, section 7):

| Scenario | Fault | Expected |
|---|---|---|
| Duplicates | `duplicate`, 3 copies | One payment and one entry; the balance rises once |
| Lost acknowledgment | `lost_response` | Delivered again, `AlreadyRecorded`, one entry |
| Out of order | `reorder` of two payments of one charge | The one handled first settles the charge, the other is flagged `charge_already_paid`; both are credited |
| A late notification of an on-time payment | `delay` past `expires_at` | `EXPIRED → PAID` |
| A late payment | A code paid after `expires_at` | Credited, flagged `charge_expired`; the charge stays `EXPIRED` |
| Slow and failing rails | `latency`, `fail` | Settled once the retries get through |
| Outage | The outage switch for a minute, while the notifications of 200 payments are due | Every payment settles once after it ends, and the inbox drains |
| Mid-settlement failure | A failure injected after the entry is posted and before commit | Nothing of the attempt remains, and the retry settles once |

The mid-settlement failure is injected in the core's database, by a deferred trigger that exists only in the test, since no fault of the rails can stop the core between two statements. A `PixCreditHandler` decorator, planned first, cannot reach that moment: the settlement opens its transaction inside the handler, bound to the charge's tenant, so a decorator runs only before it begins or after it commits.

The payer API refuses payments during an outage (rails simulator design, section 7.3), so the outage scenario makes its payments first and postpones their notifications with `delay` until the outage has begun.

The suite runs in its own Gradle task, `faultInjectionTest`, against the simulator's image built from the same commit; CI runs it on every pull request.

## 14. Trade-offs and alternatives

| Alternative | Why it was rejected |
|---|---|
| **Settle inside the callback request** | Simpler, and still one transaction, but a settlement that fails would make the rails retry, and the simulator's design already has the core answer before it settles. The inbox separates receiving from settling, at the cost of one table |
| **Judge lateness by arrival time** | `EXPIRED` would be final, but a slow notification or a retried delivery would flag a payment the payer made in time |
| **Refuse unexpected payments** | The money has already moved: the rails settle before notifying, and the core cannot reject (rails simulator design, section 4.1). Refusing would leave it in the settlement account and in no balance |
| **Refunds per charge**, drawn from the total received | A simpler request, but a Pix return refers to one `EndToEndId`, so phase 2 would have to map every refund back to payments |
| **The charge's ID as the transaction ID** | A UUIDv7 reveals the creation time, and its 26 characters do not fit subfield `05` |
| **A location URL in the BR Code**, as in official dynamic codes | Faithful, but it needs a public endpoint and a JWS signing key before any payment works; it is the simulator design's first extension |
| **A billing profile** (legal name, CNPJ, city) | Adds an endpoint, a screen and an onboarding step that no phase 1 scene needs |
| **Risk counters computed from billing's charges** | `risk` would depend on `billing`, a cycle, and a `count(*)` over a window does not stop two concurrent charges both taking the last unit |
| **Expiry by the job alone** | A payment after `expires_at` that reaches a charge the job has not expired yet would have to wait for it, or would settle the charge |
| **JPA (Hibernate)** for billing | Settlement takes explicit locks and runs in one transaction with the ledger's `JdbcClient` statements; one access style per transaction is easier to reason about, and every module so far uses `JdbcClient` |

## 15. Security considerations

Billing decides what a tenant is owed. The threats are a forged or replayed payment, money credited to the wrong tenant, a refund of more than was paid, and charges or callbacks used to exhaust the core.

| STRIDE | Threat | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Spoofing** | A forged credit notification makes a charge paid | The Ed25519 signature is verified before the body is read, and the core holds public keys only (section 7.1; rails simulator design, section 6.4); the endpoint is not routed by the public proxy |
| **Spoofing** | Someone finds another tenant's charges to pay or probe them | Transaction IDs are random, so charges cannot be enumerated; paying a charge only adds money to its owner |
| **Tampering** | The payer alters a code's amount | The payment is credited for what arrived and flagged `amount_mismatch`; the charge is not paid (section 8.2) |
| **Tampering** | A refund returns more than its payment brought in, or two refunds race | The charge's lock serializes refunds; `amount_refunded` is checked in the domain and bounded by a `CHECK` |
| **Repudiation** | A tenant disputes a payment or a refund | Each payment keeps its `EndToEndId`, acceptance time and entry; the inbox keeps the `messageId`, the `keyid` and the digest of the accepted bytes, which the simulator's copy must match; each refund keeps its correlation ID and entry |
| **Information disclosure** | Tenant A reads B's charges | The tenant comes from the API key; billing's policies are forced on every table; another tenant's charge is `404`; the isolation harness covers each operation |
| **Information disclosure** | Payer data leaks from the core | The core keeps none (section 7.2); logs carry IDs only; `metadata` must hold no personal data, as the reference says, and is never logged |
| **Denial of service** | A flood of charges, or of callbacks | The rate limits and the risk counters bound charges; the callback endpoint caps bodies at 16 KiB and is rate-limited per signing key; the processor works in bounded batches |
| **Elevation of privilege** | A developer's key refunds money | `refunds:write` is held by admins and finance members only, and a key's scopes are within its issuer's |
| **Elevation of privilege** | The expiry job changes a charge beyond expiring it | Its role's policies admit only `ACTIVE → EXPIRED`; it cannot read a paid charge, nor any column but the three it selects by |

## 17. Decisions from review

1. **Lateness is judged by the rails' acceptance time.** A payment the rails accepted before `expires_at` settles its charge however late it is notified, so an expired charge can still become paid; a payment accepted after it is credited and flagged (sections 3.2 and 8.2).
2. **A refund belongs to one payment.** A Pix return refers to one `EndToEndId`, so phase 2 sends refunds to the rails without mapping them back; the request names the payment only when the charge has several (section 10.1).
3. **Canceling a charge is deferred.** `CANCELED` arrives with its endpoint, so phase 1 builds no state that nothing reaches (section 3.4).
4. **The BR Code names the tenant's organization and the platform's city.** No billing profile is needed until a feature reads it (section 5.3).
